Palestine and the Pursuit of International Recognition and Sovereignty

Published in Summer Semester 2023

Introduction

This paper explores the complex international efforts of the Palestinian territories to gain recognition as a sovereign state. It frames the discussion in terms of international law, political recognition, and diplomacy, particularly after the Oslo Accords.

Definition of Statehood

The concept of statehood is outlined through legal and political lenses. Criteria such as defined territory, permanent population, government, and capacity for international relations are discussed, referencing the Montevideo Convention.

The Oslo Accords of 1993 and 1995

The Oslo Accords marked a significant shift in Israeli-Palestinian relations, introducing a framework for limited Palestinian self-governance. However, these agreements fell short of granting full sovereignty, leaving many key issues unresolved.

Recognition in the Global South versus the Global North

The paper highlights how countries in the Global South have been more supportive of Palestinian statehood, often as an act of post-colonial solidarity. In contrast, many countries in the Global North have remained hesitant or aligned with Israeli positions.

Recognition in Multilateral Organizations

Palestine's efforts to gain international legitimacy include seeking membership or observer status in organizations such as the United Nations and the Arab League. These moves are often symbolic but signal broader diplomatic support.

Palestine and the United Nations

In 2012, Palestine was granted non-member observer state status at the UN, marking a major diplomatic victory. Still, full membership remains blocked due to geopolitical opposition, particularly from the United States.

Palestine and the Arab League

The Arab League has long recognized Palestine as a sovereign state and supports its diplomatic efforts. However, internal divisions within the League have sometimes limited coordinated action.

International Legal Aspects of Recognition

The paper analyzes how recognition affects legal standing, treaty-making ability, and recourse in international courts. It emphasizes that recognition is both a legal and political act with real consequences for sovereignty.

Conclusion

While Palestine has achieved partial recognition, full sovereignty remains elusive. The paper concludes that international recognition is a necessary but insufficient step toward actual independence, requiring continued diplomatic engagement and internal cohesion.
